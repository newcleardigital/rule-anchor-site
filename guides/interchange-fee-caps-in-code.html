Coding the EU interchange fee caps so an auditor can check them

The Interchange Fee Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2015/751, is short and exact: a per-transaction interchange fee may not exceed 0.2 % of the value for debit cards (Article 3(1)) or 0.3 % for credit cards (Article 4). Commercial cards are outside the caps (Article 1(3)(a)). After each transaction the payee gets the merchant service charge and the interchange fee shown separately (Article 12(1)).

Four rules, four places they go wrong

Dated values, not constants

A Member State may lower a cap for domestic transactions. If the code reads "the cap" it reads today's. Store each cap with the date it took effect and look it up by the transaction's value date, and last year's fees still recompute to last year's answer.

Questions

Does the cap apply to the merchant service charge?
The caps in Articles 3 and 4 apply to interchange fees. Article 12 requires the merchant service charge and the interchange fee to be shown separately to the payee.
What about three-party schemes?
Article 1(3) also excludes transactions with cards issued under three-party schemes from Chapter II. Model the exclusion as its own requirement, with its own reading and test.
